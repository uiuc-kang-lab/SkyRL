First, I need to recall some basic facts about the parabola \( y^2 = 4x \). The standard form of a parabola that opens to the right is \( y^2 = 4px \), where \( p \) is the distance from the vertex to the focus. Comparing, I see that \( 4p = 4 \), so \( p = 1 \). Therefore, the focus \( F \) of the parabola is at \( (1, 0) \).

Next, to find the points of intersection between the line and the parabola, I can substitute \( y \) from the line equation into the parabola equation. So, substituting \( y = ax + 1 \) into \( y^2 = 4x \) gives:

\[ (ax + 1)^2 = 4x \]

Expanding the left side:

\[ a^2x^2 + 2ax + 1 = 4x \]

 bringing all terms to one side:

\[ a^2x^2 + 2ax + 1 - 4x = 0 \]

 combine like terms:

\[ a^2x^2 + (2a - 4)x + 1 = 0 \]

 This is a quadratic equation in \( x \). For the line and the parabola to intersect at two distinct points, the discriminant of this quadratic must be positive. The discriminant \( D \) of \( Ax^2 + Bx + C = 0 \) is \( D = B^2 - 4AC \).

 Here, \( A = a^2 \), \( B = 2a - 4 \), and \( C = 1 \). So,

\[ D = (2a - 4)^2 - 4(a^2)(1) \]
\[ D = 4a^2 - 16a + 16 - 4a^2 \]
\[ D = -16a + 16 \]

 For the discriminant to be positive:

\[ -16a + 16 > 0 \]
\[ -16a > -16 \]
\[ a < 1 \]

So, the range of \( a \) is all real numbers less than 1. That takes care of part (I).
